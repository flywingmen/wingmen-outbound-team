# Scoring: a number you can defend

Wingmen scores every Radar lead 0 to 10 with a written reason (`get_lead` shows it). Use this rubric when you score a hand-built list, and when you decide whether to agree with Radar.

## Points

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Pain language, explicit and recent | 5 |
| Hiring for the adjacent role | 4 |
| Funding or growth event, last 90 days | 3 |
| New in role, 1 to 3 months | 3 |
| Engaging with a direct competitor | 2 |
| Community question on the topic | 1 |

## Modifiers (they matter more than the points)

- Evidence older than 90 days: halve it
- Older than 180 days: 0
- Two or more distinct signals: +2
- Inferring instead of quoting: -1

## What the number means

- **6+** message today, the window is open
- **4 to 5** this week
- **2 to 3** watch, do not message yet
- **0 to 1** skip

## The written reason

One sentence a stranger could check, next to every score:

> **8.** Posted 6 days ago that their SDR left and they are "back to doing outreach myself". Also hiring a BD role, listed 3 weeks ago.

If you cannot write a checkable reason, the real score is 0.

## The four ways every lead ends up an 8

1. Inferring instead of quoting
2. No decay, so nothing ever ages out
3. Scoring the company, not the person (the company raised, this person may not own the budget)
4. Grading to fill a quota. If only 40 people score above 4 this week, send 40

## Recalibrate after 50 contacts

Split replies by score band. If 6+ did not beat 4 to 5, your weights are wrong for this market. Raise the signal that produced the most positive replies, lower the one that produced silence. Most markets end up with one dominant signal. `get_campaign_performance` and `get_activity` (Radar's own notes) give you the data.
